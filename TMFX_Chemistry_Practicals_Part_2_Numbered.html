    IMPORTANT NOTE ON ANIONS OF ACIDS

    HCl (aq), H2SO4 (aq), and HNO3 (aq) are strong acids. Their anions are: 〖〖Cl〗^-〗_((aq)), 〖〖 SO〗_4^(2-)〗_((aq)) and〖〖 NO〗_3^-〗_((aq)) All anions of strong acids ( that is 〖〖Cl〗^-〗_((aq)) , 〖〖 SO〗_4^(2-)〗_((aq)),〖〖 NO〗_3^-〗_((aq))) are NOT affected by dilute acids. NOTE During the test for these anions 〖〖Cl〗^-〗_((aq)), 〖〖 SO〗_4^(2-)〗_((aq)) and〖〖 NO〗_3^-〗_((aq)) dilute acids will always be added. And if any of them is present, there will be no visible change. This is because they are not affected by dilute acids.

    H2CO3 (aq) and H2SO3 (aq) are weak acids. Their anions are : 〖〖 CO〗_3^(2-)〗_((aq)) and 〖〖 SO〗_3^(2-)〗_((aq)) 〖〖 CO〗_3^(2-)〗_((aq)) and 〖〖 SO〗_3^(2-)〗_((aq))are anions of weak acids. Once a dilute acid is added to either of them, a gas will be liberated. 〖 SO〗_3^(2-) will liberate SO2 gas while 〖 CO〗_3^(2-)will liberate CO2 gas.

    Acids always liberate H2S from sulphide salts. 2HCl(s) + FeS(s) FeCl2(aq) + H2S(g) HNO3(aq) + PbS(s) Pb(NO3)2(aq) + H2S(g)

    Whenever an acid is added and nothing happens, it is because 〖 HCO〗_3^- /〖 CO〗_3^(2-) or 〖 HSO〗_3^- /〖 SO〗_3^(2-) or S^(2-) is absent. HCO3 is produced by passing excess CO2 gas into 〖 CO〗_3^(2-) or 〖OH〗^- solution.

    Sililarly, HSO3 is produced by passing excess SO2 gas into 〖 SO〗_3^(2-) or 〖OH〗^- solution. 〖 HCO〗_3^- or 〖 CO〗_3^(2-)will produce the same reactions since both of them will give off CO2(gas) once an acid is added. So if you write “〖 HCO〗_3^- or 〖 CO〗_3^(2-)”as an inference instead of 〖 CO〗_3^(2-) , you are very right.

    〖 HCO〗_3^- and 〖 HSO〗_3^- are acid salts. Their solution will always turn litmus red because of the presence of hydrogen atom in their molecule.

    I have removed 〖 HCO〗_3^- and 〖 HSO〗_3^- in this study because the current WAEC syllabus does not include them among the anions to be studied under qualitative analysis
